AristaSeptember 9, 2026

Arista Issues Critical P4Runtime Code-Injection Advisory (CVE-2026-73453)

On September 9, 2026, Arista published Security Advisory 0174 for CVE-2026-73453, a CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated code-injection flaw in P4Runtime on affected EOS platforms. A crafted packet during P4Runtime session initiation can give an attacker complete administrative control of the switch. P4Runtime is disabled by default; Arista says it discovered the issue internally and is not aware of customer exploitation. Fixes ship in EOS 4.36.2F, 4.35.6M, 4.34.8M and later trains; there is no hotfix.

Source

F5 Relevance

P4Runtime is used to program high-speed Ethernet/AI fabrics that sit next to F5 ADC, WAF, and DDoS controls. A network-adjacent RCE on the fabric switch is a joint-customer incident surface: F5 SEs should confirm P4Runtime is off or patched before treating Arista EOS as a trusted hop in BIG-IP/NGINX/Distributed Cloud designs.

IntelSeptember 10, 2026

Atsign and Intel Claim ~88x Faster Encrypted Agent-to-Agent Traffic at the Edge

On September 10, 2026, Atsign and Intel announced hardware-accelerated, end-to-end encrypted Agent-to-Agent (A2A) communications for edge AI, citing roughly 88x faster encryption on Intel Xeon (up to 5 Gbps) and ~60x on Intel Core Ultra 9 (up to 6 Gbps). The design combines Atsign's identity, namespace isolation, and auditability with Intel hardware-backed keys, accelerated crypto, secure boot, and memory/disk encryption, aimed at smart-transportation and other autonomous edge agents. Intel also added Atsign to its Industrial Builders program. Coverage is in Atsign's release and trade press including Traffic Technology Today.

Source

F5 Relevance

Encrypted A2A at multi-Gbps is an edge-AI traffic pattern F5 already handles with TLS, API gateway, and identity-aware proxy. F5 can terminate, inspect, and load-balance agent meshes that use Intel-accelerated crypto without becoming the bottleneck, and can jointly position zero-trust access for industrial/OT agent networks.
