AristaSeptember 22, 2026

Arista Issues Critical Actively Exploited VeloCloud Orchestrator Flaw (CVE-2026-93952)

On September 22, 2026, Arista published Security Advisory 0183 for CVE-2026-93952, a CVSSv3.1 10.0 improper-input-validation issue in on-prem VeloCloud Orchestrator. A remote attacker with network access to the VCO web interface and the public portion of a VeloCloud Edge authentication certificate can reach privileged internal functionality and compromise confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the orchestrator and the data it manages; tenant or operator credentials are not required. Hosted and Dedicated VCO instances were impacted and have already been patched. The issue was discovered externally and is known to be actively exploited. Affected on-prem trains include 5.2.3.15 and below, 6.1.3.7 and below, 6.4.2.7 and below, and 7.0.0.2 and below. Arista EOS switching, CloudVision, VeloCloud Gateway, and VeloCloud Edge are listed as not affected.

F5 Relevance

Joint F5+Arista branch and SD-WAN customers that still run on-prem VCO after the VeloCloud acquisition now have an actively exploited, unauthenticated control-plane CVE sitting in front of WAN, overlay, and certificate-based edge identity. F5 can help with management-plane isolation, WAF/API protection in front of the VCO web UI, TLS inspection of unexpected outbound calls from the orchestrator host, and certificate lifecycle controls for Edge-to-VCO auth.

CiscoSeptember 21, 2026

Cisco Positions Silicon-to-Optics MACsec as the Security Layer for Agentic AI Traffic

On September 21, 2026, Cisco SVP Bill Gartner argued that agentic AI is becoming the new network power user and that inference traffic is now mission-critical. Cisco research cited in the post says AI-agent tasks can generate up to 450% more WAN traffic than traditional workflows, with about 70% of that traffic from inference. The company is pushing a platform that fuses silicon, optics, systems, and software, with encryption at every layer so security does not become a latency bottleneck. The post highlights MACsec in Cisco Silicon One (including the P200) and PHY-layer encryption for scale-across AI clusters, service-provider transport, and enterprise WAN, plus quantum-resistant capabilities as cryptographic algorithms evolve. Cisco is showcasing the stack at ECOC 2026 in Málaga (September 20–24).

F5 Relevance

This is a direct competitive and partnership signal in F5's core domain: securing high-volume AI inference and agent traffic without adding latency. F5 can contrast BIG-IP, NGINX, and Distributed Cloud TLS/WAF/API controls with Cisco's silicon-and-optics MACsec story, and look for joint designs where F5 terminates, inspects, and load-balances agent and inference APIs while Cisco encrypts the optical underlay.

Dell Supplies PowerEdge XE7745 GPU Servers for On-Prem Agent AI at Korea Venture Investment

On September 22, 2026, Dell Technologies said it will supply Dell PowerEdge XE7745 4U air-cooled GPU servers to Korea Venture Investment, a public agency under Korea's Ministry of SMEs and Startups, for on-premises enterprise AI transformation. The project is framed as GPU-server purchase plus agent-AI rollout on an internal, network-separated environment so models can run under the agency's security rules. F5 can position BIG-IP, NGINX Ingress, and identity-aware access as the controlled path into Dell GPU islands used by public-sector and regulated customers that will not send source code or investment data to public clouds.

EpicSeptember 21, 2026

Royal Papworth and Cambridge University Hospitals to Share Epic via Epic Connect

On September 21, 2026, Royal Papworth Hospital NHS Foundation Trust and Cambridge University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust announced they will introduce a shared electronic patient record by extending CUH's existing Epic system to Royal Papworth through an Epic Connect model. CUH has used Epic since 2014 and is a HIMSS EMRAM Stage 7 trust. The trusts sit next to each other on the Cambridge Biomedical Campus; the shared EPR is intended to support common pathways, reduce duplication, and let patients tell their story once. Implementation is expected to take just over a year and will be delivered with Epic Systems Corporation. Leadership framed it as a clinically led move that also strengthens research collaboration.

F5 Relevance

Epic Connect expansions create multi-trust FHIR/API, identity, and session-delivery surfaces across organizational boundaries. F5 can help NHS and other health systems put API security, WAF, SSO/OIDC, and high-availability access in front of shared Epic environments so cross-trust traffic is inspected and identity-aware without slowing clinical workflows.

NVIDIA Positions Halos as Full-Stack Safety for Physical AI at Scale

On September 21, 2026, NVIDIA published a Physical AI safety briefing arguing that autonomous vehicles, humanoid robots, and industrial robots need safety across hardware, software, AI behavior, operating environment, and the full deployment lifecycle—not a one-time pre-deployment check. The post cites ABI Research (49 million Level 3–5 AVs by 2035) and Omdia (~60 million industrial robots deployed 2026–2035). NVIDIA presents Halos as the first full-stack safety system for physical AI, covering functional safety, sensor fusion, AI behavior assurance, simulation, and real-world validation, with domain-specific evidence for AVs versus robotics. It highlights context-aware safety, ISO/IEC TS 22440-style AI assurance, ongoing revalidation after model updates, and synthetic-data-scale validation.

F5 Relevance

Physical AI safety depends on trustworthy telemetry, identity, and control-plane traffic between vehicles, robots, simulation clusters, and fleet backends. F5 can position API security, mTLS, and zero-trust access in front of Halos-related agent, OTA, and validation pipelines, and treat NVIDIA's safety stack as another AI-factory traffic pattern that still needs application delivery and inspection.

Red HatSeptember 21, 2026

Red Hat Open-Sources Agentic Threat Modeling for Claude Code, Cursor, and Gemini CLI

On September 21, 2026, Red Hat Product Security open-sourced agentic-threat-modeling, an AI-assisted threat-modeling module for Claude Code, Cursor, and Gemini CLI. Built on Lola, Red Hat's open-source package manager for AI contexts, the module profiles likely attackers and writes narrative attack scenarios grounded in actual code, checked against multiple frameworks including STRIDE, PASTA, LINDDUN, and attack trees. Findings are tied to specific files and line numbers rather than generic advice. Red Hat frames it as a way for engineers to threat-model without waiting in the security-review queue as AI-generated code outpaces human review, and as structured input for later CVE reachability triage.

F5 Relevance

This is a concrete AI-safety tooling play in the software supply chain that F5 customers already run on OpenShift and Ansible. F5 can explore integrating threat-model outputs with WAF/API policy (block the reachable path, not just the CVE), and can use the same agentic-review pattern internally for NGINX, BIG-IP iRules, and Distributed Cloud policy-as-code.
