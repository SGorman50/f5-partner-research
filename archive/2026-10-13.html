Cisco October 12, 2026

NVIDIA and Cisco Introduce USFP Optics to Scale AI Factories

On October 12 at the OCP Global Summit, NVIDIA and Cisco announced Ultra-Dense Small Form Factor Pluggable (USFP), a 400 Gb/s-per-lane optical module aimed at Ethernet AI fabrics. USFP is designed for up to 12.8 Tb/s per module, about 4x the bandwidth density and 1.3x the power efficiency of 200 Gb/s-per-lane optics, so operators can connect more compute without adding network tiers. TE Connectivity and Amphenol are building a multi-source ecosystem, and the pair plan an open community specification so the form factor works across NVIDIA Spectrum-X and Cisco Silicon One Ethernet networks. In the same OCP cycle, Cisco also said Live Protect runtime vulnerability shielding will extend to Cisco-validated SONiC, with that SONiC release targeted for 2027.

Source

F5 Relevance

USFP is a new high-density optics and Ethernet fabric standard sitting on the same AI-factory networks F5 secures and load-balances. A joint Cisco Silicon One and NVIDIA Spectrum-X interconnect, plus Live Protect on SONiC, is a concrete integration surface for BIG-IP, NGINX, and Distributed Cloud in front of AI factory east-west and north-south traffic.

Cisco October 12, 2026

Cisco Advances Open AI Networking at OCP 2026

Cisco used OCP Global Summit 2026 to push an open silicon-to-Kubernetes AI network stack. Silicon One G300 (102.4 Tb/s) is already sampling with first systems shipping; a new 51.2 Tb/s G302 with 200G SerDes and direct-to-chip liquid cooling is planned to ship by the end of 2026. P200 routers, at 51.2 Tb/s with deep buffers, are in production at Microsoft, and Alibaba Cloud is aligning on SONiC and P200.

Source

F5 Relevance

Cisco's open silicon-to-Kubernetes stack, including SONiC on Silicon One and liquid-cooled top-of-rack systems, is the delivery fabric in front of AI inference APIs. F5 can pair NGINX Ingress and Distributed Cloud WAF/API security with these open Ethernet fabrics as hyperscalers and neoclouds standardize on them.

CrowdStrike ReportedOctober 12, 2026

CrowdStrike Reportedly in Advanced Talks to Buy Orion Security for About $500 Million

Calcalist (CTech) reported on October 12 that CrowdStrike is in advanced talks to acquire Israeli AI data-security startup Orion Security for about $500 million. The deal is not signed. Orion, founded in 2024, uses AI agents to watch data movement and context in place of rule-based DLP, and has raised about $38 million. The report was repeated by freely readable outlets including Runtime Wire; CrowdStrike has not confirmed it.

Source

F5 Relevance

A CrowdStrike DLP and data-movement capability aimed at AI agents expands the identity and data controls that sit beside API and application delivery. If the deal closes, F5 can position BIG-IP and Distributed Cloud API security as the in-path control for the same agent and SaaS data flows Orion would govern out of band.

Intel October 12, 2026

Intel Details Open Agentic AI Infrastructure and Crescent Island GPU at OCP

At the OCP Global Summit on October 12, Intel described open Xeon rack designs aligned to ORv3 and the GPU D-series code-named Crescent Island, an inference GPU on Xe3P with up to 480 GB LPDDR5X, 1.5 TB/s bandwidth, and 1.3 FP8 petaflops in a 350 W envelope. Intel also pointed to the AI for Enterprise Agent Toolkit. Separately, Tom's Hardware reported that Diamond Rapids will be the last Xeon to include SGX, with Intel shifting confidential computing investment to TDX, including trusted I/O and post-quantum cryptography.

Source

F5 Relevance

Intel's agent toolkit and the SGX-to-TDX shift change how confidential AI workloads are isolated. F5 can align SSL/TLS, post-quantum certificate lifecycle, and identity-aware access with TDX-based agent platforms, and treat Crescent Island inference racks as another east-west delivery target.

Nvidia ReportedOctober 10–11, 2026

Nvidia Reportedly in Early Talks to Buy or Deepen Its Stake in Reflection AI

The Financial Times reported, and freely readable outlets including CNBC TV18 and SiliconANGLE confirmed on October 10–11, that Nvidia is in early talks to acquire Reflection AI, increase its investment, or pursue an acqui-hire. No terms are set and the talks could still fall apart. Nvidia has already invested about $800 million. Reflection just launched Beam, an open-weight coding and agent model. Reported, not confirmed by Nvidia.

Source

F5 Relevance

If Nvidia takes Reflection AI's open-weight agent models in-house, inference traffic and agent tool-calls concentrate further on the Nvidia stack. That is a direct opening for F5 AI traffic management, KV-cache-aware routing, and API security in front of Beam-class agents.

Nvidia October 12, 2026

NVIDIA and Cisco Open a USFP Optics Standard for AI Factories

NVIDIA's newsroom on October 12 described USFP, a 400 Gb/s-per-lane pluggable developed with Cisco, TE Connectivity, and Amphenol, as part of a wider OCP push to open the Vera Rubin AI factory design. The same cycle includes Meta adopting Spectrum-6 102.4 Tb/s Ethernet switches.

Source

F5 Relevance

An open 400 Gb/s-per-lane Ethernet optic shared by NVIDIA Spectrum-X and Cisco Silicon One is the physical layer under multi-tenant inference. F5 Distributed Cloud and BIG-IP can sit as the security and traffic-management tier on those denser fabrics.
