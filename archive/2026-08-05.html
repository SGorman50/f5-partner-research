Arista August 4, 2026

Arista Reports Strong Q2 2026 Results and Guides Q3 Revenue to ~$3.3B on AI Networking Demand

On August 4, 2026, Arista Networks reported Q2 2026 revenue of $3.04B and adjusted EPS of $1.02, both above estimates, and guided Q3 2026 revenue to about $3.3B (vs. ~$2.94B consensus) with non-GAAP diluted EPS of $1.06–$1.08. Management attributed the upside to sustained AI-driven data-center networking demand and highlighted Arista 2.0 and 1.6 Tbps AI fabric options, including liquid-cooled designs, while expanding beyond hyperscale into enterprise campus and branch. Full-year revenue guidance was raised to roughly $12.6B (~40% growth).

Crowdstrike August 3, 2026

CrowdStrike 2026 Threat Hunting Report: AI Is Now Embedded Across Modern Adversary Operations

Released August 3, 2026, CrowdStrike’s 2026 Threat Hunting Report finds adversaries now use AI as tool, target, and force multiplier — generating payloads, abusing enterprise LLMs, and attacking AI infrastructure at machine speed (including a campaign issuing nearly 200,000 AI model requests in two minutes). The report says 88% of PoC exploits in 1H 2026 appeared within 48 hours of disclosure, AI-related cloud credential and LLM-abuse activity surged 171%, and the AI software supply chain is a primary battleground (e.g., malicious npm packages in AI frameworks). Identity abuse also accelerated, with device-code phishing attempts up 15x.

F5 Relevance

Directly validates F5 priority themes around Secure AI, API/LLM traffic inspection, identity-aware access, and software-supply-chain risk on AI data paths. Creates partnership and joint-solution angles for protecting model endpoints, agent tool traffic (including MCP), and high-velocity exploit windows with WAF, API security, and zero-trust controls.

Nvidia August 4, 2026

Open Secure AI Alliance Shows Early Progress with SAFE Working Group and Open Tooling Catalog

On August 4, 2026, TechCrunch reported that the Nvidia-spearheaded Open Secure AI Alliance — now grown to 120+ members — has already stood up the Shared AI Findings Exchange (SAFE) working group under the Linux Foundation, with open-comment proposals on confidential AI incident reporting, affected-party notification, and blame-free post-incident analysis. Members gathering at Black Hat are also cataloging open-source AI security contributions (Nvidia Garak, Okta agent identity, Red Hat agent governance, Amazon Strands Agents and Cedar). The piece frames the alliance as moving at “AI speed” one week after formation.

Highest-priority F5 signal (AI safety / Secure AI / Nvidia ecosystem). SAFE standards and open agent-security tooling are natural attachment points for F5 AI Guardrails, API gateway controls, identity-aware access, and runtime protection of agent-to-tool traffic across hybrid AI factories.

RedHat August 4, 2026

Red Hat Launches asago Open-Source Project for AI Safety and Governance Orchestration

On August 4, 2026, Red Hat announced asago (AI Safety And Governance Orchestration), an Apache 2.0 open-source community project that automates translation of corporate and regulatory AI policies into production controls. The workflow covers risk mapping (NIST AI RMF, OWASP LLM Top 10, EU AI Act via IBM AI Risk Atlas), automated safety testing, mitigation/guardrail recommendations, and deployment-ready configs for Kubernetes, Terraform, and Ansible — with a continuous audit trail from policy clause to runtime control. Collaborators include NVIDIA, Microsoft, IBM Research, MIT Lincoln Laboratory, Brave Software, and The Alan Turing Institute; the project builds on Open Secure AI Alliance work.

F5 Relevance

asago operationalizes AI safety policy into Kubernetes/Ansible-deployable controls — a direct integration surface for F5 AI Guardrails, NGINX Ingress, and API security as the enforcement plane for recommended runtime policies. Strong partnership angle with Red Hat OpenShift AI and the broader Nvidia Secure AI ecosystem.
