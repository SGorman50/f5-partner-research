Intel Agrees to Acquire Quantum Software Firm Classiq

Intel announced a definitive agreement on October 26, 2026 to acquire Israeli quantum software company Classiq and build a full-stack quantum platform that pairs Classiq's algorithm-synthesis software with Intel spin-qubit hardware. Calcalist and Haaretz report a cash-and-stock value of about $2.3 billion; Intel's own release does not disclose price. Close is expected in the first half of 2027, subject to regulatory approvals. Until then the companies operate independently, and Intel says Classiq software will stay hardware-agnostic.

Intel investor release

F5 Relevance

A full-stack quantum control plane will sit next to classical AI factories that already need API gateways, identity, and encrypted east-west paths. F5 can position BIG-IP, NGINX, and Distributed Cloud as the policy and TLS layer in front of hybrid quantum-classical orchestration APIs as those platforms leave the lab.

NVIDIA to Invest $3 Billion in Meta and Expand the Muse Agent Partnership

NVIDIA and Meta said they will deepen work on Muse and the Muse model family to improve inference and training performance as demand for AI agents grows. Meta will expand use of Vera Rubin and Vera CPUs, Spectrum-X Ethernet, and BlueField-4. The companies also flagged possible use of the OpenShell Reference Architecture and NVIDIA Sentry to deploy and monitor agents. NVIDIA will invest $3 billion in Meta's equity offering, which CNBC reports is part of a plan to raise up to $95 billion for AI infrastructure. Reported by NVIDIA's newsroom, CNBC, and Axios on October 26–27.

NVIDIA newsroom

F5 Relevance

A larger Muse agent fleet on Spectrum-X and BlueField-4 is a north-south and east-west traffic problem: token APIs, tool calls, and agent-to-agent traffic need inspection, rate limits, and identity. F5 AI Guardrails and NGINX can sit on that path alongside OpenShell and Sentry, which F5 already maps in its OpenShell reference write-up.
