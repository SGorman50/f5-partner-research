Cisco Brings Splunk AI On-Premises via AI POD with NVIDIA

On September 15, 2026 at .conf in Denver, Cisco announced Cisco AI POD for Splunk as the newest addition to Cisco Secure AI Factory with NVIDIA, so customers can run, defend, and observe Splunk AI where machine data already lives — including on-premises, private-cloud, and air-gapped environments. The Kubernetes-based POD pairs pre-validated Cisco infrastructure with NVIDIA accelerated computing and lets customers self-host selected models, with NVIDIA Nemotron models expected in coming months. Cisco also launched Tokenomics in Splunk Agent Observability to track AI-agent performance and token spend in real time, expanded agentic SOC capabilities, and formalized a multi-year AWS agreement to co-develop security solutions against AI-driven attacks.

Cisco Newsroom — Trusted AI at Scale through Splunk Advancements

CrowdStrikeSeptember 15, 2026

CrowdStrike Details PhantomRaven, an LLM-Generated npm Info-Stealer

On September 15, 2026, CrowdStrike Counter Adversary Operations published research on PhantomRaven, a JavaScript information stealer distributed via malicious npm packages and used by a financially motivated actor posing as a bug-bounty hunter. CrowdStrike assesses with high confidence that the malware was authored with a large language model, based on verbose comments, placeholder code, and token-analysis patterns. The campaign used dependency confusion and remote HTTP dependencies in install scripts to steal developer and CI/CD secrets from technology, retail, and hospitality targets.

CrowdStrike Blog — PhantomRaven

F5 Relevance

LLM-generated supply-chain malware targeting npm and CI/CD secrets is an application and API security problem, not just an endpoint one. F5 WAF, bot defense, and API gateway controls can inspect package-registry and install-script traffic, while Shape/Distributed Cloud Bot Defense helps catch automated dependency-confusion campaigns before they reach build systems.

Dell Validates Axelera Europa AIPU Systems for Enterprise and EU AI Factories

On September 15, 2026, Axelera AI launched Europa, a next-generation AI Processing Unit architecture for enterprise and data-center inference, with validated systems including Dell XE5 and Supermicro servers. SK Hynix Reported in Talks with Intel to Make Memory Chips in the U.S.

Reuters reported on September 16, 2026, citing three people involved, that SK Hynix is in exploratory talks with Intel to manufacture memory chips in the United States for the first time. Options under discussion include leasing part of Intel’s Ohio fab or forming a joint venture with Intel and major cloud providers; no decision has been made. Intel called the reports speculation while confirming it is still investing in the Ohio site. The talks sit against U.S. pressure to onshore chip production and SK Hynix’s lead in HBM used next to AI processors. CNBC coverage of the same report is login-gated; The Next Web republished the Reuters account without a paywall.

The Next Web (via Reuters) — SK Hynix / Intel talks

F5 Relevance

A U.S. HBM/DRAM footprint tied to Intel foundry and cloud customers would concentrate more AI-factory memory supply onshore. F5’s interest is the resulting AI-cluster networking and security surface — GPU-to-HBM fabrics still terminate on Ethernet, APIs, and management planes where BIG-IP, NGINX, and Distributed Cloud already play.

NVIDIA Shows DSX Tokens-per-Watt Gains and New AI Factory Partners

At the AI Infra Summit on September 15, 2026, NVIDIA highlighted DSX MaxLPS factory-wide power optimization, claiming up to about 1.4x more tokens per megawatt, with Lambda reporting 23% better performance per watt. Red Hat and Arqit Demo Confidential Containers with Quantum-Safe Networking

On September 15, 2026, Red Hat published a joint technical blog with Arqit showing how confidential containers plus quantum-safe networking can keep SaaS customer data under customer control on untrusted public cloud.
