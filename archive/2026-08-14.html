Crowdstrike August 13, 2026

CrowdStrike Expands Project QuiltWorks to SMBs, Extending Frontier AI Risk Protection to Organizations of Every Size

On August 13, 2026, CrowdStrike said it is extending Project QuiltWorks — its coordinated frontier-AI risk program — from enterprises to SMBs worldwide. The expansion uses distributors and channel partners including Arrow Electronics, Ignition Technology, Nord Security, Pax8, TD SYNNEX, Westcon-Comstor, and Zip Security to deliver AI-driven vulnerability discovery, expert-led remediation, and financial protection on the Falcon platform. QuiltWorks is powered by frontier models from OpenAI and Anthropic and pairs CrowdStrike prioritization with SI remediation, AWS-hardened infrastructure, and cyber-insurance partners. CrowdStrike CBO Daniel Bernard framed frontier AI as shifting cybersecurity from risk management to a business imperative for organizations of every size.

F5 Relevance

Frontier-AI risk is moving down-market through the same channel that already sells F5. QuiltWorks creates a partner conversation on how F5 AI Guardrails, WAF, and API security sit in front of SMB and mid-market AI apps that Falcon now covers — especially where OpenAI/Anthropic traffic and AWS-hosted agents need inspection, identity-aware access, and DDoS protection.

Dell August 13, 2026

Protect Your AI Workloads to Drive What’s Next

On August 13, 2026, Dell published a cyber-resilience briefing arguing that attackers now target AI models, training/inference pipelines, and recovery paths — not just traditional applications. The post positions Dell PowerProtect Data Manager with PowerProtect Data Domain as the layer that protects every tier of the AI stack so enterprises can recover models, pipelines, and data with confidence. The message treats AI workloads as a first-class backup and recovery domain rather than a bolt-on to existing VM protection.

F5 Relevance

Protecting AI pipelines implies high-volume, latency-sensitive data paths between GPU clusters, object stores, and backup appliances. F5 can sit on those S3 and API flows with BIG-IP/NGINX for TLS, access control, and inspection — and jointly position AI traffic security plus PowerProtect recovery as a full-stack AI resilience story.

RedHat August 12, 2026

Red Hat Links Agentic AI Infrastructure to Platform Control and Agent Sandboxes

On August 12, 2026, SiliconANGLE published a CUBE Conversation with Joe Fernandes, VP/GM of Red Hat’s AI Business Unit, on why agentic AI is shifting enterprise focus from model choice to platform control. Fernandes cited exploding token costs and data-sovereignty risk as reasons to stop relying exclusively on public-cloud AI services, and said platform teams must now treat agents as the next class of enterprise application. Red Hat is extending its open-source model into agent sandboxes and NVIDIA OpenShell as a secure runtime so organizations can govern what autonomous agents can access on the network and filesystem.

F5 Relevance

Agent sandboxes and OpenShell put identity, network reachability, and runtime isolation at the center of production AI — the same control plane F5 already owns with BIG-IP, NGINX Ingress, and AI Guardrails. A joint OpenShift + F5 reference for sandboxing agent egress, inspecting MCP/tool calls, and enforcing zero-trust access would map directly to this Red Hat narrative.
