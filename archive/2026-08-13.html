Cisco August 11, 2026

Secure Claude Enterprise with Cisco AI Defense

On August 11, 2026, Cisco detailed how Cisco AI Defense integrates with Anthropic Claude Enterprise via inference hooks to inspect every governed prompt before the model runs. Claude, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork act on user behalf—reading documents, calling tools, and executing tasks—so prompts become an attack surface for jailbreaks and prompt injection. AI Defense returns an allow/deny verdict so malicious prompts never reach the model, without requiring infrastructure changes, and Cisco is evolving the path toward native Inspect API enforcement.

F5 Relevance

Runtime prompt inspection for agentic enterprise assistants is core F5 AI Guardrails / API security territory. This validates a gateway-style pre-inference control plane F5 can complement or co-sell beside Cisco AI Defense and Anthropic deployments—especially for customers already routing AI traffic through F5 BIG-IP, NGINX, or Distributed Cloud.

Cisco August 12, 2026

Cisco Reports Fourth Quarter and FY 2026 Earnings; AI Infrastructure Orders Hit $9.3B

Cisco reported Q4 FY2026 revenue of $17.3 billion (+18% YoY) and full-year revenue of $63.3 billion (+12% YoY), exceeding guidance with record margins. Q4 product orders rose 35% YoY (25% ex-hyperscalers), networking product orders grew 40% YoY for an eighth straight double-digit quarter, and AI infrastructure orders reached $4 billion in Q4 / $9.3 billion for FY2026, with ~$4B AI revenue delivered in FY2026 and $7.5B expected in FY2027. Management framed a broad networking supercycle alongside hyperscaler AI demand.

F5 Relevance

Cisco’s AI infrastructure order ramp and networking supercycle signal sustained enterprise and hyperscaler spend on AI fabrics, secure networking, and observability—markets where F5 competes and partners on app delivery, API security, and AI traffic management. Earnings commentary is a useful BD signal for joint AI factory and Secure AI positioning versus Cisco’s expanding AI stack.

Dell August 10, 2026

Deploying Muse Glimmer on Dell Pro Precision — Local Agentic AI with Meta

On August 10, 2026, Dell published guidance for deploying Meta’s Muse Glimmer, an open-weight (~30B) agentic multimodal model under Apache 2.0, on Dell Pro Precision workstations and Dell Pro Max with GB10. Muse Glimmer is designed for local-first autonomous workflows—reasoning, planning, tool use, and multi-stage tasks without data leaving the device—and is available via Dell Enterprise Hub on Hugging Face with optimized packages. The post positions Dell client/workstation hardware as the foundation for private, on-device agentic AI development and production-like local runs.

F5 Relevance

Local open-weight agentic models expand the edge/client AI attack and policy surface F5 already addresses in data-center AI factories. As Muse Glimmer agents call tools and enterprise APIs from Dell endpoints, F5 can secure north-south API paths, enforce identity-aware access, and extend AI Guardrails patterns from cloud inference to hybrid local-agent + enterprise-backend architectures.
