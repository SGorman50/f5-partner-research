Dell · October 13, 2026

Michael Dell Says AI Models Need Safeguards and a Government Role

In a CNBC Squawk on the Street interview on October 13, Dell CEO Michael Dell said AI models need safeguards and bounded controls, and that government has a role alongside companies. He did not spell out a policy proposal. A follow-up clip on October 14 restated a long-term optimistic view that the industry will still be here in 2030. The remarks sit next to calls from Anthropic and OpenAI to pace frontier development, and next to political pushback on data-center buildout ahead of the 2026 midterms.

CNBC

F5 Relevance

Dell's public stance on model safeguards and bounded permissions is a sales opening for F5's AI traffic controls: WAF and API security in front of inference endpoints, identity-aware access for agents, and policy enforcement on the path between Dell AI Factory nodes and enterprise apps. F5 can pair that narrative with BIG-IP and Distributed Cloud as the network control plane that makes safeguards and controls concrete for Dell joint customers.

Nvidia · October 13, 2026

NVIDIA Commits $250 Million to UK Agentic Safety and Security Research

NVIDIA said on October 13 it will donate compute valued at $250 million over five years, about $50 million a year, to UK agentic safety and security research. Most of the capacity goes to the wider UK research community, with a share for BOLD at Oxford, SOFAIR at UCL, and Imperial College London. Access is delivered through UK-based NVIDIA Cloud Partners so the compute stays in-country. Jensen Huang framed it as advancing capability and safety together. The program points at secure agents, open-model security, threat mitigations, and sandboxes, and it references NVIDIA OpenShell and Sentry designs.

NVIDIA Newsroom

F5 Relevance

A funded UK agentic-safety program built on OpenShell and Sentry is a concrete place for F5 to attach API gateway, mTLS, and WAF controls on the path between research agents and model endpoints. NVIDIA Cloud Partner delivery also means inference traffic will cross multi-tenant fabrics where F5 Distributed Cloud and NGINX can enforce identity-aware access and rate limits without sitting inside the GPU node.
