CrowdStrikeOctober 13, 2026

CrowdStrike Closes XM Cyber Technology Deal at About $586 Million

Ctech reported on October 13 that CrowdStrike has closed its purchase of XM Cyber technology assets from Schwarz Group for about $586 million: roughly $145 million previously disclosed plus 2,118,022 CrowdStrike shares worth about $441 million at close. The deal covers source code, IP, and more than 45 patents, not XM Cyber's revenue or customer base. XM Cyber continues as a standalone business under license to CrowdStrike, with a partnership angle toward Schwarz Digits' STACKIT sovereign cloud. Same-day follow-ups report large Israeli layoffs as the operating company restructures. This is separate from the still-unsigned Orion Security talks already briefed on October 13.

Source

F5 Relevance

Attack-path IP landing inside Falcon expands the continuous exposure-management surface CrowdStrike can attach to identity and agent controls. F5 can position BIG-IP, NGINX, and Distributed Cloud as the inline enforcement layer for the paths XM Cyber models — API, WAF, and identity-aware access decisions that close the gaps a simulation finds.

Intel and Qualcomm Reportedly in Talks to Buy Tenstorrent

The Index Today reported on October 13 that Intel and Qualcomm are both circling Tenstorrent, Jim Keller's Toronto RISC-V AI-chip startup, with reported valuations from about $5 billion to $10 billion. Reuters previously reported Qualcomm talks in June at $8–10 billion (The Information). Startup Fortune (October 10) said Intel is also in early talks and that Tenstorrent is running a dual track of a sale and a new funding round. No agreement is signed. Reported/rumored; primary wires are paywalled, so the link is the freely readable recap.

Source

F5 Relevance

A Tenstorrent deal would pull inference accelerators and an open RISC-V software path into Intel's AI-infrastructure story, next to the IPU and Xeon networking work F5 already watches. If Intel owns more of the inference box, F5's angle is the delivery and security tier in front of those racks — load balancing, API gateway, and TLS for multi-tenant inference — rather than competing on the chip.

Red HatOctober 13, 2026

Red Hat and Ferrous Systems Put Safety-Qualified Rust on In-Vehicle OS

Red Hat announced on October 13 a collaboration with Ferrous Systems to bring the Ferrocene safety-qualified Rust toolchain onto Red Hat In-Vehicle Operating System (RiVOS). Ferrocene is TÜV SÜD-qualified for ISO 26262 up to ASIL D. Ferrocene 26.05 already targets aarch64-rhivos2-linux-gnu so teams can develop and test now; a jointly certified solution is planned for the second half of 2027.

Source

F5 Relevance

Safety-qualified Rust on an enterprise Linux vehicle OS is a new software-supply-chain and runtime surface, not a core F5 product, but the same customers will expose vehicle and factory APIs that need WAF, mTLS, and certificate lifecycle controls. F5 can pair NGINX and BIG-IP with Red Hat's automotive stack as the north-south security tier for those connected-vehicle services.
