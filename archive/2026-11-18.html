NVIDIA · November 17, 2026

NVIDIA Reports $115.8 Billion Q3 Revenue as Vera Rubin NVL72 Begins Shipping

NVIDIA reported fiscal 2027 third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103 percent year over year and 20 percent sequentially, for the quarter ended October 25, 2026. Data Center revenue was $107.6 billion, up 110 percent year over year. GAAP diluted EPS was $2.70. Interim CEO Colette Kress said Vera Rubin NVL72 has shipped and called it the fastest product ramp in company history. Fourth-quarter revenue is guided to $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3 percent, with no China Data Center compute revenue assumed.

Source

F5 RELEVANCE

The Rubin ramp and a $130 billion quarterly outlook set the pace for every AI factory design F5 sells into. Delivery, API security, and identity controls need to be in the reference architecture while those racks are being installed, not added after the cluster is live.

NVIDIA · November 16, 2026

Lambda Adopts NVIDIA Photonics Co-Packaged Optics for AI Factory Networks

NVIDIA said cloud provider Lambda is using NVIDIA Photonics co-packaged optics switches to make large GPU training networks faster, more resilient, and more power-efficient. The November 16 post frames the problem as thousands of GPUs that have to stay in lockstep, so the network itself becomes part of model-training performance.

Source

F5 RELEVANCE

Co-packaged optics changes loss, latency, and failure modes on the AI fabric. F5’s role stays on the north-south and service edge of those factories: TLS, API gateway, and DDoS controls in front of inference and agent endpoints.

ARISTA · November 17, 2026

Arista Expands Etherlink With R4 Platforms for Secure Distributed Inferencing

Arista announced an expanded Etherlink R4 portfolio for agentic and chained AI inference across distributed data centers. New 7020HR4/7020R4 front-end routers, 7282R4 and 7280R4 coherent transport platforms, and a 25.6 Tbps pluggable optical line system add MACsec/IPsec, SRv6 in silicon, and multi-tenancy. Arista says the 5nm designs use up to 60 percent less power. Most platforms are available now; the 7020HR4 is planned for early 2027.

Source

F5 RELEVANCE

Distributed inference creates a new front-end routing and encryption surface between metro and edge sites. F5 can pair BIG-IP and Distributed Cloud security with these fabrics for TLS inspection, API protection, and consistent policy on inference traffic routed closer to users.

RED HAT · November 17, 2026

Red Hat and Microsoft Bring OpenShift Virtual Machines to Azure

SiliconANGLE reports that Red Hat and Microsoft are expanding their partnership so enterprises can run virtual machines on Azure with Red Hat OpenShift Virtualization, using Azure’s direct virtualization for near bare-metal access, including GPUs. Red Hat would be the first commercial Microsoft partner to support OpenShift Virtualization on Azure Red Hat OpenShift. The work extends KubeVirt with Microsoft’s hypervisor. No general-availability date was given.

Source

F5 RELEVANCE

OpenShift Virtualization on Azure pulls VM and container ingress onto one Kubernetes control plane, including GPU workloads. That fits NGINX Ingress, F5 Distributed Cloud, and BIG-IP virtual editions as the delivery and WAF layer across those mixed workloads.

RED HAT · November 17, 2026

Azure Red Hat OpenShift Earns IL5 Authorization for Defense Workloads

ExecutiveBiz reports that Azure Red Hat OpenShift on Microsoft Azure Government has moved from IL4 to IL5, so U.S. defense agencies can buy it through the Joint Warfighting Cloud Capability vehicle. The authorization sits alongside FedRAMP High and FIPS 140-2/140-3.

Source

F5 RELEVANCE

IL5 OpenShift on Azure Government is a regulated ingress and API path. F5 can position FIPS-capable BIG-IP and NGINX controls, plus certificate lifecycle management, in front of that cluster.

INTEL · November 16, 2026

Intel’s SC26 Note Puts Silicon Photonics Next to an AI-Ready HPC Stack

In a November 16 newsroom essay timed to Supercomputing 2026, Intel argued that HPC should stay open and heterogeneous, pairing Xeon 6 and upcoming Diamond Rapids CPUs with PCIe GPU D-series accelerators for on-system inference. The piece also describes silicon photonics as the way to move accelerator and memory traffic over longer distances at lower energy. It restates the October 26 proposed Classiq acquisition and does not add new deal terms.

Source

F5 RELEVANCE

Optical links between accelerators and memory change where AI traffic is terminated and secured. F5 should watch these designs for places where BIG-IP and NGINX still sit in front of heterogeneous clusters.
