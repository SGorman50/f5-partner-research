August 31, 2026

AMD, Cisco and HUMAIN Expand Saudi Arabia AI Infrastructure as AMD Instinct Systems Go Live

On August 31, 2026, AMD, Cisco, and PIF-backed HUMAIN announced that AMD Instinct MI355X GPU-based AI infrastructure built on Cisco Silicon One networking is now in production in Saudi Arabia, delivering GPU-as-a-service for training and inference. The fabric uses Cisco N9000 Series switches and 800G optics to interconnect MI355X GPUs with AMD EPYC CPUs. The partners plan up to 250 MW of additional AI infrastructure starting in 2027 and remain on track for up to 1 GW by 2030, framed as an open, sovereign AI platform.

September 1, 2026

Diraq and Equinix to Deploy Australia's First Quantum Computer Inside a Data Centre

On September 1, 2026, Diraq and Equinix announced plans to install Australia's first quantum computer in a commercial Equinix data centre in Sydney, described as the first silicon-spin quantum system in a shared commercial facility worldwide. The eight-qubit system is self-contained, draws less than 20 kW, and is designed to sit beside standard servers with open network connectivity for remote monitoring and hybrid quantum-classical work next to AI/GPU racks. Installation is expected to complete in October 2026, after which partners can explore applications in an Equinix facility that already meets enterprise sovereignty and compliance requirements.

Source

F5 can engage Equinix on secure access, TLS/PQC-ready delivery, and API controls for remote quantum-classical workflows that will share Equinix Fabric with GPU clusters.

NVIDIA Invests $3.5 Billion in MediaTek and Expands NVLink Fusion for Custom XPUs

On August 31, 2026, NVIDIA and MediaTek announced a deeper edge-to-cloud AI partnership. MediaTek will adopt NVIDIA NVLink Fusion so hyperscalers and frontier-model developers can build custom XPUs that plug into NVLink-connected, rack-scale AI factories, spanning cloud AI infrastructure, local AI PCs, and automotive. NVIDIA also invested $3.5 billion in MediaTek convertible bonds, its largest direct investment outside the U.S. TechCrunch and Bloomberg covered the deal as NVIDIA's answer to Big Tech building its own AI silicon while remaining the rack-scale interconnect and software standard.
